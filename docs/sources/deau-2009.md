# Opposition-surge shapes of Enceladus and Rhea: Deau et al. (2009) (`deau-2009`)

**Paper:** Deau, E., Dones, L., Rodriguez, S., Charnoz, S. & Brahic, A. (2009). The opposition effect in the outer Solar system: a comparative study of the phase function morphology. *Planetary and Space Science* 57, 1282–1301, DOI [10.1016/j.pss.2009.05.005](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.pss.2009.05.005). Text source: the accepted manuscript arXiv:0902.0345v1, fetched with sha256 recorded.

**Corrigendum:** *Planetary and Space Science* 161, 137 (2018), DOI [10.1016/j.pss.2018.03.006](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.pss.2018.03.006). ScienceDirect answers 403, so its content is unknown here. This is an open issue.

**What it is:** morphological fits (logarithmic, linear-by-parts, linear-exponential) to published disk-integrated opposition phase curves of satellites and rings. The Saturnian satellites among them:
- Enceladus: Verbiscer et al. 2005, *Icarus* 173, 66, HST, 439 nm. The data span about 0.25–20° (paper Fig. 2).
- Rhea: Domingue et al. 1995, *Icarus* 115, 228, and Verbiscer & Veverka 1989, *Icarus* 82, 336, about 500 nm.
- Iapetus (trailing side) and Phoebe.

**Transcribed:** `pipeline/src/pipeline/photometry/tables/saturnian_opposition.json` holds, for Enceladus and Rhea:
- the Table 3 linear-exponential parameters (p. 61, checked against a 220 dpi rendering): I/F = I_b + I_s·α + I_p·exp(−α/2w), paper Eq. 5, α and w in degrees;
- the Table 3 logarithmic fits;
- the Table 2 linear-exponential A, HWHM and S (p. 60).

The table prints the slope as a positive number, while Eq. 6 defines S = −I_s. The fitted slope is therefore negative. This is confirmed against the logarithmic fit within 6 % over 0.5–6°; a positive slope would differ by more than 20 % at 6°.

**Checks** (`tests/test_photometry_moons.py`): Table 3 reproduces Table 2 through Eq. 6, with A = (I_p + I_b)/I_b and HWHM = 2 ln2 · w, to ≤ 0.01.

**Use:** `photometry.json` → 601–605 `phaseFunction` below 10° (label **estimated**):
- The formula is Φ(α) = Φ_VIMS(10°) · R(α)/R(10°), where R is the linear-exponential fit.
- Enceladus and Rhea use their own fits. Mimas, Tethys and Dione use the mean of the two (an assumption: no accessible measured curve for them).
- The fits describe observed curves, which include the smoothing by the Sun's finite disk as seen from Saturn.

**Caveats:**
- The fits' absolute scale (I/F = 10^(−0.4M), Eq. 1) is not used; only the shape is.
- Transferred to the VIMS level at 10°, the surge-inclusive albedos come out 1.2–1.4× below the HST true-opposition albedos (Verbiscer et al. 2007). The Enceladus curve shows that this is a difference in absolute level between the data sets, not a missing part of the surge (`docs/reports/planet-colors.md`, M3).
- The leading-hemisphere curves are used for whole-moon brightness.
