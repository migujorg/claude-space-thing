# Wang et al. (2024) Saturn full-disk reflectance from Cassini ISS (`wang-2024`) — digitized

**Citation:** Wang, X., Li, L., Jiang, X., Fry, P. M., West, R. A., Nixon, C. A., Guan, L., Karandana G, T. D., Albright, R., Colwell, J. E. et al. (2024). Cassini spacecraft reveals global energy imbalance of Saturn. *Nature Communications* 15, 5045. DOI [10.1038/s41467-024-48969-9](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-024-48969-9). Open access (CC BY 4.0). The PDF and its Supplementary Information were fetched from nature.com; their sha256 values are in the download ledger.

**What the paper measured:** Saturn's full-disk reflectance against phase angle from Cassini ISS global images taken in 2004–2017, in seven filters:
- **Image selection:** only images with sub-Cassini latitude < 3°, so that the rings hide little of the disk.
- **Calibration:** CISSCAL 4.3.
- **Normalization:** each reflectance is divided by the solar irradiance after ring shadowing and ringshine (the "ring-modified" irradiance).

The authors fitted a four-order polynomial in phase angle to each filter's ISS points together with Karkoschka's (1998) ESO full-disk reflectance at 5.7° (their Fig. 7). They found no significant change over the mission, only 1–3 % (Supplementary Figs. S13–S14). They tabulate neither the data nor the coefficients; both are "available from the corresponding author on request".

## Digitized

`pipeline/src/pipeline/photometry/tables/wang_2024_saturn_fig7.csv` holds the fitted curves of Fig. 7 panels A (RED, 647 nm), B (GRN, 568 nm) and C (BL1, 463 nm).

- **Method:** the figure is a 300 ppi raster on page 6 of the PDF. The curves were read column by column as the mean row of the green line's pixels. Each panel's tick marks calibrate the axes; for GRN, 0.2 in reflectance is 86 px and 60° is 125 px.
- **Precision:**
  - the digitized line scatters by 0.0006 rms (about 0.25 px) about a smooth four-order polynomial;
  - the pipeline refits the paper's four-order form (`phase.wang_saturn_poly`) and uses only ratios of the curve;
  - the ESO points digitize to 0.562 / 0.509 / 0.373. Karkoschka's table, band-averaged over the WAC filters, gives 0.538 / 0.498 / 0.363. The figure's absolute normalization therefore differs by 2–4 %, and that difference does not enter.
- **Coverage:** the last ISS point is at 161° (RED, GRN) and 146° (BL1). The curves beyond are the paper's extrapolation, so the app tabulates only to 160°.

## Use: Saturn's phase function in `photometry.json`

- **0–5.7°:** Mallama & Hilton (2018) Eq. 11, which borrows Jupiter's polynomial (an assumption, unchanged).
- **5.7–160°:** the GRN curve relative to its value at 5.7°, joined to Eq. 11 there. The join is exact in the data: the app's Saturn albedo is Karkoschka's 1995 ESO spectrum at 5.7°, the same point the paper anchors to.
- **What it replaces:** Eq. 12, a fit to the red-light Pioneer 11 scattering model of Dyudina et al. (2005). Integrating the Dones et al. (1993) Barkstrom law over the disk reproduces it to 1–4 % (30–150°), so Eq. 12 is in effect Pioneer's photometry. The Cassini curve is fainter than Eq. 12 by these factors:

| Phase angle | Eq. 12 / Cassini |
|---|---|
| 30° | 1.28 |
| 54.6° | 1.36 |
| 60° | 1.35 |
| 90° | 1.23 |
| 120° | 1.10 |

  The shape differs even between ISS points alone, with no ESO anchor involved: from 17° to 55°, the ISS curve falls by a factor 1.18–1.23 more than Eq. 12.
- **Label:** estimated. Reasons:
  - Eq. 11 below 5.7° is an assumption;
  - the curve is digitized from a figure;
  - one filter's curve serves all channels (RED and BL1 differ from GRN by −2 % and −6 % at 54.6°);
  - the join depends on the relative calibration of ESO (±4 %) and CISSCAL.
- **Independence from the validation set:** the Saturn validation frame (Cassini WAC, 2016-04-25) was taken from 28.7° above the ring plane. The paper keeps only images taken within 3° of the ring plane, so the frame is not in its dataset. Both use CISSCAL, so a common error in the ISS absolute calibration would not show in the comparison.
