# Uranus narrow-ring centrelines, support and estimated mean-width bands

Primary fit: French, R. G., Hedman, M. M., Nicholson, P. D., Longaretti, P.-Y. and McGhee-French, C. A. (2024), *The Uranus system from occultation observations (1977–2006): Rings, pole direction, gravity field, and masses of Cressida, Cordelia, and Ophelia*, Icarus 411, 115957, [DOI:10.1016/j.icarus.2024.115957](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.icarus.2024.115957), [arXiv:2401.04634v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.04634v1). Table 5 (manuscript pp.26–28) is transcribed, including formal one-sigma errors, in `french_2024_uranus_ring_orbits.csv`; gamma uses the solution including m=3. Table 6 contains separately fitted normal modes. Table 14 (p.45) supplies nine mean widths, with no width-error column. They are transcribed in `french_2024_uranus_ring_support_widths.csv` and are not recomputed from rounded edge differences.

The epoch is TDB 1986-01-19 12:00. The COR is the geometric centre, not the centre of opacity or mass. Eq.1 gives an ellipse with independently fitted constant apsidal and nodal rates. Longitudes start at the ascending node of Uranus's angular-momentum equator on Earth's J2000 equator, opposite the IAU north-pole sense. Fixed eccentricity/inclination terms remain zero; their undefined apse/node has no inferred orientation. Their errors are unavailable, not zero. Table 5 supplies marginal errors, not the full covariance needed for an exact propagated standard deviation.

Support comes from [French et al. (2023), *Uranus ring occultation observations: 1977–2006*, Icarus 395, 115474](https://authors.library.caltech.edu/records/vr3cc-p4838/files/1-s2.0-S0019103523000519-main.pdf?download=1), §§4.1,4.29–4.30/Table 5, and French 2024 Tables 8/10. Accepted COR support starts 1977-03-10 and ends 2006-09-20 for 6/5/4/alpha/beta/gamma/epsilon. Eta and delta end 2002-07-29 (their U0602 profiles were excluded). Lambda's eight candidates run 1985-05-04–1996-04-10. These are ring-specific COR detection spans; the publications do not give separate min/max dates for every edge or mode. The pipeline applies the COR support conservatively to the historical component at whole-day TDB precision, with the existing positive-width gate. The cutoff is a pipeline policy, not an author warranty.

Lambda has no paired-edge fit or Table 14 width. Its 2.3 km review width comes from [PDS RMS Vital Statistics for Uranus's Rings](https://pds-rings.seti.org/uranus/uranus_rings_table.html), citing Nicholson, de Pater, French and Showalter (2018), *The rings of Uranus*, in *Planetary Ring Systems*, pp.93–111. No uncertainty is specified. Its COR radius has 1.314 km formal error and 3.477 km fit RMS; its circular location and opacity remain particularly tentative. The review's epsilon optical-depth range is longitude variation, not a mean or uncertainty: it is not averaged to invent a mean tau. Estimated bands instead carry the slice's historical PPS equivalent depth uniformly over the published width, labelled estimated. Reflectance, including lambda's unknown brightness, is unchanged.

At Best/Complete outside support, a constant-width band uses the propagated COR ellipse. No independent edge fit, observed normal mode, or longitude-dependent width law is extended. Static m=0 offsets ±W/2 encode its boundaries in the existing component arithmetic; they are not measured oscillation modes. Method text lists omitted COR mode amplitudes (gamma's m=0 alone is 5.509 km), and epsilon's omitted historical width variation of about 20–97 km. Each method computes data-window endpoint errors from Table 5: `sigma_phase = hypot(sigma_angle0, delta_days*sigma_rate)` and displacement `ae*sin(sigma_apse)` or `a*sin(i)*sin(sigma_node)`. These are diagonal phase-only one-sigma statistical proxies conditional on constant rates. Missing covariance, radius/amplitude/pole errors, omitted modes, unknown libration and later dynamics prevent a total physical error bound. Undefined angular terms stay explicitly undefined.

[French and Souami (2023), *Earth-based Stellar Occultation Predictions for Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune, Titan, and Triton: 2023–2050*, PSJ 4,202, DOI:10.3847/PSJ/aced50](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.13530), §§3,5.3/Table 4, publishes forward occultation predictions using ring orbits through 2050. This is the prediction practice the temporal estimate follows, not a measurement or a guarantee of unchanged dynamics. Its planning widths differ from the final French 2024 widths and are not used here.

The PDF page images and Eq.22 were checked; no silently corrected width-law parameter enters the product. Width variation cannot be inferred from COR elements alone.
