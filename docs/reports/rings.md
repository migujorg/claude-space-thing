# Giant-planet ring profiles and components

The `light` stage builds `rings.json` from cited downloads and transcribed tables. This report describes the component slice; Saturn's calibrated model and checks remain in [planet-colors.md](planet-colors.md). No rendered-scene result is claimed here. The models enter Best estimate and Complete; Strict withholds the estimated component models, retaining the measured Uranus/Neptune occultation cuts with unknown brightness. All aspect labels and input IDs are in the product; raw-file hashes resolve through `sources.json`.

## Saturn archive and reconstruction

Saturn now has two separate sourced attributes. `opticalDepth` is measured: every archived Cassini UVIS β Cen 2008-231 τ is retained, including signed noise and the outer rise, with archive τ=-1 or note-flag bit 64 represented as null. `opticalDepthEstimate` is estimated: the existing `clean_saturn_tau` output rounded to four decimals, with the same observation geometry and maxTau lower-limit data. Best and Complete prefer the estimate through the ordinary label-admission rule; Strict falls back to the archive. Missing bins keep the existing unknown-material handling.

The reconstruction detects the inner main-ring edge as the first run of at least 3 bins with τ > 5 photon σ and the outer edge as the last run of at least 20 bins with τ > 0.2. Inside those inclusive edges it clamps every -1 < τ < 0 to zero, regardless of significance. Outside, it separately subtracts each segment's running median (window min(201, segment_length | 1), SciPy nearest endpoint padding; nominal 2010 km). Only runs of at least two adjacent residuals strictly > 3σ survive as max(residual,0); every other outside bin becomes zero, including isolated significant positives and negative residuals. These thresholds and exact zeros are assumptions. The smooth beyond-F rise is **assumed** instrumental; no independent calibration or source substantiating that cause is supplied. The median includes F-ring material and can remove extended real opacity. The product method gives the conditional photon-noise formula, guards and diagnostics; it does not account for star/background-model systematics or reconstruction uncertainty. Validation has not selected or altered these rules.

The fitted reflectance model continues using the cleaned reconstruction through `saturn_profile`; its `normalTau` and dependent HST inversions, Voyager modulation and unlit fits inherit `estimated`. Its numerical fitting and shader arithmetic are unchanged. Strict excludes that estimated brightness, so positive measured outer opacity produces extinction and unknown-reflectance material marking, not calibrated outer-ring radiance; Best removes the smooth outer opacity via the estimate.

Neptune's PN1P01 archive marks 40 bins at 51,200–51,395 km unconstrained (mean signal 0 or opacity lower limit -9): they are now null, rather than measured zeros. Uranus's PU2P01XE array is unchanged from the archive. The archive comparisons and level selection are covered by pipeline and CPU app tests; no GPU/eye-model result is claimed by this change.

## Geometry support and crossed edges

[French et al. 2024 §4.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.04634v1) explicitly fit IER, COR and OER separately, including separate precession and mode speeds at one common reference epoch. Section 6 discusses uniform precession as the dynamical equilibrium ideal and a centreline width law, while retaining anomalous fitted edge rates (notably γ). The tabulation is an empirical edge fit, not a supplied rigid-ring ephemeris. Replacing those rates or choosing a width law to make today's widths positive would add a new model assumption without a fitted dynamical solution.

The defect was unrestricted extrapolation of those fits followed by an uncited 0.5 km width floor. The floor is removed. The product carries `geometryValidity`, with a basis explicitly naming the pipeline's no-extrapolation policy: Table 8's 1977-03-10 through 2006-09-20 observation dates, represented at whole-day TDB precision. This is observation support, **not an author-stated future validity warranty**. Outside support, or at any nonpositive/nonfinite local width, CPU geometry is null and GPU geometry is unknown. That component supplies zero light and extinction. Hatching covers a conservative radial annotation envelope, not a replacement physical band; unsupported arcs likewise have no predicted longitude. Positive widths have no floor.

Sampling the actual manifest window (ET 796998128–891692528, 2025–2028) at 101 times and 720 longitudes gives these raw, unsupported minimum widths in km:

Ring 4's edge apsidal-rate difference is 0.0003424°/day, accumulating 4.903–5.278° from epoch across this window; γ's is 0.0007670°/day, accumulating 10.983–11.824°, with additional independently fitted modes. Nonpositive widths occur in 2,883 and 7,854 samples respectively. A second 101 × 720 sample over the observation-support interval finds γ widths down to -1.693 km (1,500 nonpositive samples), while ring 4 stays above 1.303 km. The empirical multi-mode fit is not a globally constrained physical width law even inside its supporting interval; the positive-width gate therefore remains necessary there. These are sampled diagnostics, not exhaustive minima or validation targets. All ten narrow components are now withheld throughout this unsupported window, including ones whose extrapolated width happens to be positive. The app regression reads the manifest rather than fixing these dates in its test.

[Souami et al. 2022 §3.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2110.12669v2) infer 820.11178 ± 0.00003°/day from the 1989 Voyager and 2016 SPHERE longitudes. The slice formerly propagated the 2016 morphology indefinitely. Current-window dates are 3,145.95–4,241.95 days beyond that reference, or 7,166.75–9,663.54 rotations. Combining the 0.30° longitude error with the rate error in quadrature gives 0.314–0.326° formal longitude error; that omits morphology evolution and is not a future validity claim. The same no-extrapolation defect is fixed: arc support ends at the light-time-corrected 2016 reference. Inside the anchors, use of the 2016 shape at other dates remains estimated. The four non-arc Neptune bands are stationary, with positive widths 2,000, 100, 3,950 and 15 km. All six Jupiter components are stationary; widths are 5,361.9, 13,226.02, 22,800, 52,200, 92,700 and 18,100 km. Neither system has independently drifting band edges to fix.

## Limits and independent checks

Uranus μ and ν are missing. Arago is merged into Lassell. ζ uses a Voyager 1986 radial profile whose structure differs from 2007; it remains an explicitly estimated stationary profile. The arcs retain their 2016 morphology within their motion-support interval. No evolutionary interpolation is supplied. Torus extinction and inclined-plane extinction are omitted; unresolved component light is discarded without a tested perceptual bound. Jupiter's halo continuation to 50,000 km and radial height taper are assumptions, not detections at those bounds.

Calibration and independent checks are distinct. Uranus ε's JWST NEW selects its albedo by construction; other measurements test it. Remaining disagreements include predicted inner-ring NEW around 545 m versus measured 376 m, model Bond albedo 0.0448 versus Voyager 0.014 ± 0.004, Jupiter model predictions around 0.544 and 0.524 of two high-phase Galileo points, and Adams PPS equivalent depth 0.756 ± 0.129 km versus a review's 0.045 km. Discrepancy tests record these gaps; they do not close them or tune a factor to pass. GPU compilation, scene placement, hatching, absolute radiance and torus integration convergence require root's hardware review.
